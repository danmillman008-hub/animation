Out in the dusty town of Dry Gulch, there lived a young gunslinger called Rusty, who reckoned he was the fastest draw in the whole territory.
Every morning he strutted down Main Street, spinning his pistol and telling anyone who'd listen just how quick he was.
Now the sheriff of Dry Gulch was a calm woman named June. She never hurried, and she never bragged.
Rusty laughed at her. "Sheriff, you're slower than a mule on a Sunday! Bet you couldn't hit a barn if you were standing inside it!"
June just tipped her hat. "Talk is cheap, kid. Tin cans on the fence, at high noon. Let's see who's really the best shot."
The whole town turned out. Rusty fired fast, whooping and hollering, and knocked down two cans in a flash.
He was so sure he'd won that he holstered his gun, sat down in the shade of the saloon porch, and pulled his hat over his eyes for a little nap.
June took her time. She breathed slow, aimed careful, and one by one... every single can went flying.
Rusty woke to the sound of cheering, but it was too late. The sheriff had cleared the fence, and the whole town was hollering her name.
Rusty scratched his head and grinned. "Well, I'll be. I reckon fast ain't the same as good." And June just smiled. "Slow and steady, kid. Slow and steady."
